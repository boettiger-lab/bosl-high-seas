High Seas Explorer work in progress

An interactive map and AI assistant for exploring marine protected area candidates, fishing effort, seafloor geomorphology, and ocean governance across the high seas — the ocean beyond national jurisdiction.

← Open the App

About this project

A project of the Eric and Wendy Schmidt Center for Data Science & Environment (DSE) at UC Berkeley, in partnership with the Benioff Ocean Science Laboratory (BOSL) at UC Santa Barbara.

Roughly two-thirds of the ocean lies beyond any country's Exclusive Economic Zone — the high seas, or Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction (ABNJ). Under the 2023 BBNJ "High Seas Treaty", the global community can now establish marine protected areas in these waters for the first time. BOSL provides spatial and scientific analysis to support States developing high seas MPA proposals; this app lets researchers, negotiators, and the public explore candidate MPA boundaries alongside the data that matters for evaluating them — fishing effort, seafloor habitat, existing protections, ecoregions, and the patchwork of regional fisheries governance.

This is a work in progress — layers, models, and capabilities are being added and refined.

How it works

This app is powered by geo-agent, DSE's open-source framework for AI-assisted geospatial exploration. Open datasets stream straight to your browser from cloud-native formats — no tile server in between — and an AI assistant answers questions by writing live queries against the same data. Full technical details are in the geo-agent documentation.

🌱 Low-carbon, open models. Be patient. While much faster than most humans, the geo-agent makes use of more environmentally-friendly AI models. (Check out the carbon dashboard 🌱 to see the CO₂/energy use of this deployment.)

Exploring the map

The map is built on MapLibre GL — the open-source engine behind the familiar Mapbox-style interactive maps. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, and hold Ctrl and drag to change the aspect — tilting and rotating the view, which is useful for reading seafloor depth and terrain in 3D. The globe projection lets you spin the whole planet, handy for the high seas, which wrap all the way around it.

The Overlays panel lists every data layer. Toggle a layer on or off with its checkbox, and hover any feature for a tooltip of its live attributes — names, categories, areas — straight from the source data. When layers overlap, use the send-to-back button (↩) in the Overlays header to drop the topmost layer beneath the others; click it repeatedly to cycle through and peek at whatever is hidden underneath.

Asking the assistant

The Data Assistant understands plain English and does two kinds of things: it can drive the map for you, and it can answer analytical questions about the data. Just type what you want — if it can't do something, it will say so and usually suggest the closest thing it can.

Map operations happen right away on the layers already loaded — no hunting through menus:

  • Filter a layer: "Show only no-take marine protected areas."
  • Recolor or restyle a layer: "Color marine protected areas by IUCN category", "Make fishing effort 50% transparent."
  • Fly somewhere: "Fly to the Mid-Atlantic Ridge", "Zoom to the South Pacific."
  • Show, hide, or step through time: "Turn on Marine Ecoregions", "Show fishing effort in 2015."

Analytical questions are answered by writing live spatial queries over the underlying data, returning a number or a downloadable table:

  • "What is the combined area of all high seas MPA candidates?"
  • "How does 2024 fishing effort vary across seafloor geomorphological features?"
  • "If every MPA candidate were implemented, how many hours of 2024 fishing effort would fall within their boundaries?"
✏️ Draw an area to ask about it. Use the draw tool on the map to sketch a polygon — a proposed reserve, a region you care about, anywhere on the globe — then ask the assistant about it: "How many hours of fishing effort fall inside the area I drew?", "What seafloor features does this polygon contain?", "What share of this area overlaps existing marine protected areas?" Your drawn shape becomes a live spatial filter for any query.

Data layers & sources

Every layer draws from a public, primary source. Click "STAC" beside any entry to see the full machine-readable metadata — schemas, column descriptions, license, lineage, and download URLs.

Protected Areas

High Seas MPA Candidates
Candidate marine protected area boundaries proposed for Areas Beyond National Jurisdiction under the BBNJ Agreement — the primary analytical targets of this app. Each polygon carries the proposal's lead body, status, area category, and supporting parties.
About BBNJ proposals: The 2023 Agreement on Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction (the "High Seas Treaty"), adopted under UNCLOS, creates the first legal mechanism to establish area-based management tools — including marine protected areas — in the two-thirds of the ocean beyond any country's jurisdiction. Under the treaty, States or groups of States submit proposals delineating candidate areas, the conservation objectives they would serve, and the measures (such as fishing or shipping restrictions) they would carry; proposals are reviewed by a Scientific and Technical Body and decided by the Conference of the Parties. The candidates shown here represent areas that have been advanced or identified through this and allied scientific prioritization efforts.
STACSource: BBNJ Agreement
BBNJ proposals — compiled from publicly proposed candidate areas
Marine Protected Areas (WDPA)
World Database on Protected Areas — the most comprehensive global record of marine and terrestrial protected areas, a joint project of UN Environment Programme and IUCN managed by UNEP-WCMC. Filtered here to marine and coastal realms, styled by IUCN management category.
STACSourceLicense
Protected Planet terms — non-commercial, attribution (UNEP-WCMC & IUCN)

Fishing Effort

Global Fishing Watch Apparent Fishing Effort (2012–2024)
Annual apparent fishing effort derived from AIS vessel tracking for >190,000 vessels, aggregated to H3 hexagonal cells by gear type (fleet-monthly dataset v3.0, released March 2025). Selectable year-by-year from 2012 through 2024.
STACSourceLicense
CC BY-NC 4.0

Maritime Boundaries

High Seas (ABNJ) & Exclusive Economic Zones (EEZ)
IHO / Marine Regions World EEZ v12 and World High Seas v2. EEZs define 200-nautical-mile zones under national jurisdiction; the High Seas layer is the complementary ocean area beyond any national EEZ — the foundational reference for distinguishing ABNJ from national waters.
STACSourceLicense
CC BY 4.0 — Flanders Marine Institute (VLIZ)

Ocean Governance

RFMO Boundaries & VME Closures
Food and Agriculture Organization of the UN — Regional Fisheries Management Organization jurisdictional boundaries and Vulnerable Marine Ecosystem closures, where bottom-contact fishing is restricted to protect cold-water corals, sponge grounds, and seamounts. The primary fisheries-governance framework for the high seas.
STACSourceLicense
CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO — FAO

Ocean Biogeography

Marine Ecoregions of the World (MEOW)
Spalding et al. 2007 — a bioregionalization of the world's coastal and shelf areas into 232 ecoregions nested within 62 provinces and 12 realms, the standard reference for assessing the representativeness of MPA networks. Covers coastal and shelf waters only.
STACSource
The Nature Conservancy / WWF — free for non-commercial conservation use

Seafloor

Seafloor Bathymetry (GEBCO 2025)
General Bathymetric Chart of the Oceans 2025 Grid — a global terrain model at 15 arc-second (~450 m) resolution, sub-ice variant. Negative values are depth below sea level. Produced by GEBCO / British Oceanographic Data Centre.
STACSourceLicense
GEBCO Grid terms — freely available, attribution (doi:10.5285/8896b933-5744-4bdf-862b-43a76c3f91a8)
Seafloor Geomorphology
Global Seafloor Geomorphic Features Map (Harris et al. 2014) — 152,079 polygons classifying seamounts, ridges, abyssal plains, trenches, canyons, shelves, and 20 other feature types. Distributed by Blue Habitats / CSIRO.
STACSourceLicense
CC BY 4.0 — Harris et al. 2014, doi:10.1016/j.margeo.2014.01.011
